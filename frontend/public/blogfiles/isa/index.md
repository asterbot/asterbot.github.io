
I want to write about what it was like to be on the teaching side at UWaterloo. I often call this experience one of the most influential experiences in my early career and I fondly look back to it. For a long time it was my favorite co-op experience and I really miss that time.

First things first: I was working as something called an ISA (Instructional Support Assistant). If you haven't heard that term before, it's basically an undergraduate TA. I got to work directly on the frontline, talking to students, working with profs and generally getting to help in the process of how courses are run. All good fun! 

I was teaching a course with the code [CS136L](https://uwflow.com/course/cs136l) and often helping out with [CS136](https://uwflow.com/course/cs136). These courses are there to help first years get familiar with C programming, programming with a some cool data structures and algorithms, and the lab course is to help students get better at learning some very important tools for being a good developer (eg. bash, Makefiles, debugging tools, C/C++ preprocessors kinda stuff) and in my opinion that sets students up for success if they take the course seriously enough. 

## Thoughts on teaching during this time

I did this back in January-April 2024, which feels like forever ago. I had done a bit of volunteering teaching here and there, and a bit of non-volunteering teaching work, I'm not getting into that though.

In terms of teaching things in Math/CS, I feel like a big part of learning these kinds of subjects is being able to feel like you could've discovered it by yourself. I really love the "aha!" moments I get when I figure something out which I found relatively complex, and that's the kind of experience I hope I was able to give the students I taught. This is something I learned from 3B1B, who is my absolute GOAT. While this sounds fine in theory, I was often trying to figure out how to even give a hint that doesn't give the answer but does guide them in the right direction. It is a skill I had to learn, which honestly forced me to understand the content a lot better. 

## Non-teaching things I did that co-op

So strangely this was also a pretty software *development* heavy co-op. UWaterloo uses an auto-grading system to mark student submissions (aka [Marmoset](https://marmoset.cs.umd.edu/)) in the lab and in the course, where the students submit their solution files (`.c`, `.sh` or whatever) to the university servers and some grading scripts assign them a score. 

Before I *worked* on this system I was somehow pretty fascinated by how it worked. You just submit a file, and boom, you got a score! Back then this kinda felt like magic, so being able to actually work on this stuff was so insanely cool to me.
